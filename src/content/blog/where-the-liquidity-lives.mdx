When we [measured Robinhood Chain](/blog/measuring-robinhood-chain) before
building on it, one finding did the most work: the stock tokens live in Uniswap
pools, and **if you are buying, depth is the number that matters, not price.**
On a concentrated name you trade close to the quote; on a thin one, the gap
between the price you see and the price you get is the whole cost of the trade.

So we built the page that shows it. [Pools](/pools) is a live map of every
Uniswap v4 pool that pairs a stock token with USDG on chain 4663 — grouped by
stock, sorted by depth, and honest about what can and cannot be measured. This
is how it works and how to read it.

## How the liquidity is arranged

A Robinhood stock token trades against **USDG**, a stablecoin, in Uniswap v4
pools. That much is simple. The part that surprises people is that a single
stock does not have *a* pool — it can have several, and they are rarely equal.

Each pool is created at a **fee tier**: 0.05%, 0.30%, 1.00%, and non-standard
tiers besides. Anyone can create one, so a token accumulates pools the way a
street accumulates shops — some busy, most empty. NVDA is the clean example. As
of writing it has **four** pools:

| NVDA pool | Fee | Depth to 2% |
|---|---|---|
| 0.30% | routes here | **$203,976** |
| 0.05% | | $1,112 |
| 1.00% | | $256 |
| 1.00% | | $4 |

Four pools, and **one of them holds 99% of the tradeable liquidity.** The other
three are real contracts with real (tiny) balances that will happily quote you a
terrible price. This is what fragmentation looks like on this chain, and it is
why the page never just shows "NVDA's price" — it shows *which* pool a trade
would actually route through, and how deep that one is.

## Why we show depth, and not TVL

The instinct is to show TVL — total value locked, dollars in the pool. We
don't, for one honest reason: **in Uniswap v4 you cannot read it per pool.**

v4 keeps every pool's tokens in one shared vault (the `PoolManager` singleton).
A `balanceOf` tells you how much of a stock token sits across *all* its pools
combined, and the USDG side cannot be attributed back to any single pool at all.
So a per-pool dollar TVL is simply not a number the chain will give you without
guessing, and we would rather show you a true number than a plausible one.

What we show instead is **depth**: how many dollars you can trade into a pool
before its price moves by a set amount. The page uses two thresholds — a green
band up to a 0.5% price move, and an amber band up to 2%. It answers the only
question a buyer actually has: *how much can I put through here before it starts
costing me?* Depth is computed from the pool's live state (its price and its
active liquidity), the same math behind the depth gauge in the [swap](/swap)
flow. It is an informative estimate, not an execution guarantee — a pool can be
deeper or thinner a block later — and the page says so.

The one dollar figure we *can* show honestly is **pooled value (stock side)**:
the stock tokens a pool holds, times their price. It is roughly half of a full
TVL — the USDG half is the part the singleton hides — so we label it plainly and
show it per stock, never per pool.

## How to read the page

The table is one row per stock, sorted by depth, deepest first. Left to right:

- **Stock** — ticker and name. Click the row to expand it.
- **Pools** — a dot per active pool. Two dots means two pools worth showing.
- **Best fee** — the fee tier of the *deepest* pool, the one a trade realistically
  routes through.
- **Price** — the token's price, tagged with its source (a Chainlink feed where
  one exists, otherwise the pool's own spot price).
- **Pooled value** — the stock-side dollars in the pools, marked *stock side* so
  you never mistake it for full TVL.
- **Depth to slippage** — the hero. A two-tone bar: the green portion is what you
  can trade before a 0.5% move, the amber before 2%. The bar is scaled to the
  deepest stock on screen, so the longest bar is the market's deepest pool and
  everything else is read against it.

**Click any row to open it.** The pools underneath are the fee tiers, each with
its own price and its own depth, and the deepest one is flagged *routes here*.
This is where NVDA's story above becomes visible: expand it and three of the
four pools are visibly empty. You also get each pool's *spacing* — the
granularity of its price grid, tied to the fee tier, a detail for the curious
and safely ignorable otherwise.

Above the table, four counters summarise the whole market: total depth to 2%,
active pools, how many of the 96 stocks have any liquidity at all, and — in
amber — how many have none. As of writing that last number is the honest
headline: **around $654k of depth spread across 36 pools, but only 19 of 96
stocks have a live pool.** The market is real, early, and concentrated, exactly
as the research said. Sort by *fragmentation* to find the stocks whose depth is
scattered across tiers rather than pooled in one.

## What we actually built

Almost nothing new had to be measured. Our backend already reads every pool's
price and liquidity on every sync cycle to price the tokens — it just kept the
single deepest pool and threw the rest away. The Pools page is mostly a matter
of *keeping* what we were already reading: we now store each pool's depth and
serve it, grouped by stock, from one public endpoint. Every figure on the page
is read from chain 4663, live; refresh it in a minute and the numbers move.

That is the same posture as everything else we build here. Liquidity on this
chain is a public object — its depth a number anyone can read, not a
relationship you have to ask an intermediary about. A page that shows it plainly,
and admits what it can't show, is just that posture applied to the thing that
decides whether a trade is a good one. Go [look](/pools).
